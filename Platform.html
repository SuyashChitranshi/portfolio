01

The Problem: Navigating Existing Fragmented Processes

Users struggled to navigate and understand the existing workflow due to fragmented information, unclear process states, and inconsistent interaction patterns.

As workflows grew in complexity, users found it difficult to track progress, identify blockers, and confidently complete tasks, leading to increased dependency on manual follow-ups and stakeholder clarification.

From a business perspective, this resulted in inefficiencies, delayed decisions, and higher risk of operational errors.

03

Key Design Decisions

Decision #1

Unification of Disparate Workflows

The most significant decision was to consolidate disconnected tools—such as emails, spreadsheets, and manual documents—into a single centralized ecosystem. This eliminated the "fragmented workflow" pain point by housing employee evaluations, timesheet tracking, and delivery monitoring in one place, which reduced the time users spent manually gathering updates across various platforms.

01. Cognitive Load Reduction

Resulted in Faster task completion and improved confidence in system usage.

02. Inline Actions

Allowed users to resolve issues directly from the dashboard without navigating to details.

Decision #2

Transition to Structured Forms and Unified Actions

The design team replaced cluttered, inconsistent layouts with structured forms and simplified timesheets. This decision directly addressed the 71% of PMO members who previously struggled with inconsistent reporting formats. By implementing unified workflow actions, the platform achieved a 72% reduction in form errors and inconsistencies
